# Reference games

Why each game matters to Frontier, mostly from Andrew's own account of what kept him playing and what made him stop.

| Game | What's relevant | Watch for |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Disco Elysium | Skills as ways of seeing, checks set by the situation, failure as content, a small dense world, a mystery that holds to the end. Completed and adored. | The model for progress through understanding |
| The Sims | People with their own skills and relationships; long single-life play | Drops off once money, skills and hidden abilities are all earned |
| Crusader Kings | A custom nobody in an unknown land, a world moving around you | Too punishing as a nobody; sudden death; snowballing; heirs |
| Flight Simulator 2020 + Neofly | The whole world to explore; bush flying; finding places | Places with no state; careers snowball away from the small details |
| Course of Temptation / Degrees of Lewdity | Text lets the world say yes to almost anything; permanence | Depth runs only through the player; little NPC-to-NPC life |
| The Witcher / Baldur's Gate 3 | Rich stories | Dropped once the build was complete; the mechanical curve ends before the story |
| Citizen Sleeper | Single character, text, precarity without combat | Scarcity fades mid-game; the last stretch is just tying off threads |
| RimWorld | NPC relationships drive drama; fun early game | Multiple characters; tech scales quickly |
| Wildermyth | Procedural, text-told character stories | Changing characters; party play |
| Caves of Qud (Roleplay mode) | Rich systemic world | Not as a roguelite: no replaying or lost progress |
| Kenshi / Dwarf Fortress | A nobody in an indifferent world; a world history that moves on its own | Survival focus and lethality; setting and graphics |
