# Design pillars

The reasons Frontier exists. Every system is tested against them. Most come from Andrew's account of why he drops the games he otherwise enjoys.

1. **One character, one continuous life.** You play a single character the whole way. No party, no heir, no switching. Character progress is never lost to a reset. **[Decided]**
2. **A world that moves without you.** People have their own routines, skills and relationships with each other, not just with you. Places change whether or not you are there. **[Decided]**
3. **Progress through understanding, not power.** You grow by learning how the world works. Investigation is the core loop. Growth widens what you can notice and do; it does not shrink what can challenge you. **[Decided]**
4. **Never outgrow the small details.** The scrappy early phase is the game, not a tutorial to escape. If a constraint such as supplies fades in importance, a different pressure must replace it. Guard against snowballing at every scale. **[Decided]**
5. **A mystery that opens as you climb.** The peak mystery should answer one question by raising others, and only close at the summit. Running out of things to discover is a failure. **[Decided]**
6. **Precarious, not lethal.** Danger is real but the character doesn't die. Failure costs time, standing and supplies, not the save. No survival grind. **[Decided]**
7. **Permanence without godhood.** Your actions leave lasting marks on places and people, but you don't dictate the world. **[Decided]**
8. **Easy to pick up, deep to master.** Simple pieces, deep combinations. **[Decided]**
9. **Choices and state drive play; words are flavour.** Lean text, with richer description available when you look closer. Text should still evoke feeling, and acknowledge progress. **[Decided]**

## Tests applied to every system

- Does it stay interesting once the player has mastered it, or does it run out? **[Decided]**
- Does success at it make the next challenge trivial (snowballing)? **[Decided]**
- Does it only respond to the player, or does the world use it too? **[Decided]**
- Can it end or ruin a save suddenly? If so, it fails pillar 6. **[Decided]**
- When capability grows, does consequence grow with it? The late-game question moves from "can I?" to "what does this cost, and to whom?" **[Proposed]**
